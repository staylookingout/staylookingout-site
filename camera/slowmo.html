How this works

Conforming footage shot at a high rate down to your timeline rate stretches it in time by shoot ÷ edit. Shoot at 120 and drop it on a 24 fps timeline and everything plays 5× slower. So a performance filmed at that rate needs its music running 5× faster on set — after the slowdown it returns to normal tempo, still locked to the lips and the beat.

Playing a track faster also raises its pitch (tape/record spun faster). When you slow the footage back down, that pitch drops by the exact same amount — so the exported varispeed file is what actually re-lands on pitch in the final cut. Keep original pitch is offered for previewing here, so you can recognize the song while you check timing; it doesn't change the downloaded file.
